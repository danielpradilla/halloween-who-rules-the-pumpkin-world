The countries growing the most pumpkins aren’t necessarily the ones selling the most abroad.

What counts as a pumpkin here

These figures include pumpkins, squash and gourds of all varieties, not just the orange Halloween pumpkins. Zucchini, butternut squash and kabocha count too.

Production is in tonnes and exports are in US dollars, so I only compare ranks and shares. The numbers cover the whole year, not just Halloween.

Possible reasons (not tested)

  • People eat them at home? India and China have many people, and they may eat much of what they grow. FAOSTAT does not count this, so I cannot check.
  • Distance to buyers? Pumpkins are heavy, cheap and do not keep long. Mexico borders the United States, and Spain is in the EU.
  • Growing season? Mexico and southern Spain may be able to grow pumpkins when buyers elsewhere need them.
  • The trade code? HS 070993 counts only Cucurbita species. Bottle gourds, common in South Asia, use another code. So India’s exports may be undercounted.

Sources & methodology

Where the numbers come from and how I used them

Production: FAOSTAT

FAOSTAT Crops and livestock products (QCL), file updated . Item 394 “Pumpkins, squash and gourds”, element 5510 Production, in tonnes, for .

The item covers pumpkins, squash, zucchini and bottle gourds, whatever they are used for. FAO marks each number as official ( countries), estimated () or imputed by FAO (). The table under the first chart shows the mark for each country.

Exports: UN Comtrade

UN Comtrade, through its public API. Product code HS 070993 “Pumpkins, squash and gourds (Cucurbita spp.), fresh or chilled”. Exports to all countries, as reported by the exporter, for .

Values are in US dollars at the exporter’s border, without shipping costs. They include re-exports when a country counts them. This may inflate the numbers for trading hubs such as the Netherlands.

How I calculated the numbers

Production share is a country’s tonnes divided by FAO’s world total (). The countries add up to less than that total. FAO does not explain the difference.

Export share is a country’s exports divided by the total of the countries that reported ().

A country without a number has no rank. I never count it as zero. “Exports weigh X% of what it grew” divides Comtrade weight by FAOSTAT tonnes. It is approximate, because the two sources do not count exactly the same products.

Gaps and limits

FAO’s “China” total includes mainland China, Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan. I left it out and ranked China (mainland) on its own, so nothing counts twice. Regions such as “Asia” are left out too.

FAOSTAT has no number for . Among the top 20 growers, did not report exports to Comtrade. The page shows them as “not reported”.

For those countries, I added up what other countries said they bought from them. The largest is , against for the 10th exporter. I used these numbers only for this check and for the top buyers on the country card.

FAOSTAT counts bottle gourds (Lagenaria). The trade code does not. The trade code also counts only fresh or chilled produce.
