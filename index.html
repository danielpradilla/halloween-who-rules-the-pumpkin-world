Who Rules the Pumpkin World?

The countries growing the most pumpkins aren’t necessarily the ones selling the most abroad.

What counts as a pumpkin here

These figures include pumpkins, squash and gourds of all varieties—not just the orange pumpkins traditionally carved for Halloween. Courgettes, butternut squash and kabocha count too.

Production is measured in tonnes and exports in US dollars, so the two are only compared by rank and by share of the world total. The trade figures cover a full calendar year; they are not Halloween sales.

Takeaway

Why production leaders and export leaders differ

In these figures, a country’s production rank says little about its export rank. The first list is calculated from the data on this page. The second gives explanations that annual production and trade totals cannot test.

Plausible explanations, not tested here

  • Domestic demand. India and China have large populations that eat pumpkins, squash and gourds as everyday vegetables. FAOSTAT’s food balance sheets do not list this item separately, so this page cannot show how much of the harvest is eaten at home.
  • Market access and distance. Fresh squash is heavy, perishable and cheap per kilogram, which favours suppliers close to large importers. Mexico borders the United States, and Spain is a truck ride from France, Germany and the United Kingdom.
  • Off-season supply. Mexico and southern Spain can grow courgettes and squash in winter, when buyers further north have little local supply. Annual figures cannot show this seasonal pattern.
  • The trade code’s scope. HS 070993 covers only Cucurbita species. Bottle gourds (Lagenaria), common in South Asia, are traded under another code, so India’s gourd exports may be understated here.

Sources & methodology

Datasets, definitions, calculations and limitations

Production: FAOSTAT

Dataset
FAOSTAT Crops and livestock products (QCL), bulk file updated .
Item
394 “Pumpkins, squash and gourds” (CPC 01235): Cucurbita species (pumpkins, squash, courgettes) and Lagenaria gourds.
Indicator
Element 5510 Production, tonnes, calendar year .
Definition
Harvested crop quantity, whatever its use. FAO marks each value as official ( countries), estimated () or imputed (); the flag is shown for each country.

Exports: UN Comtrade

Dataset
UN Comtrade, annual goods trade, retrieved through the public preview API.
Code
HS 070993 “Pumpkins, squash and gourds (Cucurbita spp.), fresh or chilled”. The subheading exists in HS 2012, 2017 and 2022 with the same wording. Reporters used HS 2022 (), HS 2017 () and HS 2012 ().
Indicator
Exports (flow X) to all partners combined, as declared by the exporting country. Value is FOB in current US dollars, calendar year .
Definition
Goods leaving a country’s economic territory, including re-exports where the reporter counts them. That matters for trading hubs such as the Netherlands.

Calculations

Production share
Country tonnes ÷ FAO’s World total (). Country rows sum to , less than the World total; FAO’s aggregates can include values not published at country level.
Export share
Country export value ÷ the sum of exports declared by all reporting countries (). Non-reporters are not in the denominator.
Ranks
Production ranks among countries with a FAOSTAT value; export ranks among Comtrade reporters. A country with no reported value has no rank; it is never treated as zero.
Exported weight ÷ production
Comtrade net weight ÷ FAOSTAT tonnes, shown on the country card. Approximate: the two classifications differ in scope, and re-exports can push the ratio above 100%.

Coverage and joins

Aggregates
FAO regional aggregates and its “China” total (mainland, Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan) are excluded from rankings; China (mainland) is ranked on its own, so nothing is counted twice.
Missing values
FAOSTAT lists as missing for . Among the top 20 producers, had no export declaration in Comtrade. These appear as “not reported”, not zero.
Coverage check
For countries that did not report exports, imports declared by their partners were summed. The largest, , compares with for the 10th-ranked exporter, . These partner-declared figures are used only for this check and for the “main buyers” on the country card; they are never mixed into the rankings.
Joining the sources
FAO M49 area codes are matched to Comtrade reporter codes; six differ (United States 840/842, France 250/251, India 356/699, Switzerland 756/757, Norway 578/579, and Taiwan 158, published by Comtrade as “Other Asia, nes” 490).

Scope differences

FAOSTAT’s item includes Lagenaria bottle gourds; HS 070993 does not (they fall under 070999). HS 070993 covers fresh or chilled produce only; frozen, dried or processed pumpkin is classified elsewhere. Production is a physical quantity and exports are a value, so the page compares ranks and shares rather than putting tonnes and dollars on one axis.
